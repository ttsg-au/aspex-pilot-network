1. Getting started

The Aspex Flight Logbook keeps your drone flight records in one place, online, so they aren't stuck on one computer. It's free for members of the Aspex Pilot Network.

Your logbook is yours

You can log all your flying here, not just Aspex work. Aspex only sees the flights you fly on Aspex jobs. Everything else stays private to you.

Which drones work best

The logbook works best with DJI drones, which get full support: flight times, GPS tracks, battery health and charge cycles. senseFly eBee and Skydio are supported in part. See section 3.

Signing in

Aspex sends you an invitation email with a Set your password button. After that, log in any time at the logbook's address with your email and password. Bookmark it. If you forget your password, ask Aspex to send you a reset link.

Phone, tablet or computer

You can look at your logbook, flights and CASA logs on any device. Importing flights from a controller is easiest on a computer: the Import Flights tool needs a Windows PC, and dropping files in works on any computer.

Your first 10 minutes

  1. Set your password from the invitation email.
  2. Fill in the Me tab: your name, ARN and RePL details. Your CASA logs and reminders depend on it.
  3. Import your flights (section 2).

2. Getting your flights in

All importing starts with the blue Import flights button at the top right of the logbook. It opens a window where you can:

  • drop in your flight files (works from any computer)
  • sync your Skydio flights
  • download the Import Flights tool, an optional shortcut for your drone PC that copies new flights off the controller and sends them in for you
  • move your history across from another logbook (under Settings and moving data across)

The button itself doesn't reach into your controller. The flight files still need to come from somewhere: either you drop them in, or the Import Flights tool sends them for you.

Nothing gets doubled up. Flights already in your logbook are recognised and skipped, so it's always safe to drop in a whole folder or import the same files again.

On a Windows PC

Before you start

  • Use the USB cable that came with your controller, or a good-quality USB-C data cable. Some charging-only cables won't show the files.
  • Have your drone switched on as well as the controller. With most DJI controllers, the flight files only appear on the PC while the drone is on and linked.

The easy way: the Import Flights tool (DJI)

One-time setup: in the logbook, click the blue Import flights button, then click Download Import Flights and save it to your desktop. You only need to do this once on each PC.

Then, each time:

  1. Plug the controller into the PC with the USB cable.
  2. Switch the drone on and wait for it to link to the controller.
  3. If the controller asks how to use the USB connection, choose File transfer.
  4. Open Import Flights on your desktop.
  5. It finds the new flights on the controller, sends them to your logbook, and opens the logbook for you. It closes itself when it's finished.

If it says it found no files, check the drone is switched on and linked, then open Import Flights again.

The manual way: drop the files in

Use this on a PC without the Import Flights tool, or if you'd rather do it yourself.

  1. Plug in the controller and switch the drone on, as above.
  2. Open File Explorer and click on your controller in the left-hand list.
  3. Open Internal shared storage → DJI → com.dji.industry.pilot → FlightRecord
  4. In the logbook, click the blue Import flights button.
  5. Drag the flight files (named like DJIFlightRecord_2026-11-09_….txt) from File Explorer into the box marked Drop files or click to choose.

That folder is for DJI Pilot 2, used with the Mavic 3 Enterprise series and the Matrice drones. Older DJI drones flown with the DJI GO 4 app keep their files in DJI → dji.go.v4 → FlightRecord on the phone, tablet or controller.

On a Mac

Macs can't read DJI controller files over USB without extra software, and the Import Flights tool is Windows only. There are two simple ways.

Using a microSD card (nothing to install)

  1. Put a microSD card in your controller.
  2. On the controller, open the Files app (sometimes called File Manager).
  3. Go to Internal storage → DJI → com.dji.industry.pilot → FlightRecord
  4. Select your flight files and copy them to the SD card.
  5. Put the card in your Mac (with an adapter if needed).
  6. In the logbook, click the blue Import flights button and drag the files from the card into the box.

Using a USB cable and OpenMTP (free)

  1. Download and install OpenMTP from openmtp.ganeshrvel.com.
  2. Connect the controller with a USB-C cable and switch the drone on. If the controller asks, choose File transfer.
  3. Open OpenMTP. The controller's storage appears on the right.
  4. Go to DJI → com.dji.industry.pilot → FlightRecord and drag the files to your desktop.
  5. In the logbook, click Import flights and drag them into the box.

Skydio

Skydio flights don't need files. They come straight from Skydio Cloud.

  1. In Skydio Cloud, create an API token for your account.
  2. In the logbook, click Import flights, open Settings and moving data across, paste the token into the Skydio box and click Save.
  3. Click Sync Skydio flights. Run it again whenever you've flown. Flights already in your logbook are skipped.

The DJI developer key

Newer DJI flight files are encrypted. A free DJI developer key lets the logbook unlock them, so you get GPS tracks and battery data. Without it, flights are still logged, but without the track or battery details. As an Aspex Pilot Network member, you don't need one: your imports use Aspex's key automatically, and there's nothing to set up.

Moving your history from Drone Logbook

This is a one-off job, separate from importing new flights. It works for any brand of drone, because it uses Drone Logbook's own export.

  1. In Drone Logbook, go to Report / Import → Import / Export (CSV).
  2. Click Export next to each of these and save the files: Flights, Drones, Battery and Locations. Flights is the important one. The others turn Drone Logbook's codes into your aircraft, battery and site names.
  3. In this logbook, click Import flights, open Settings and moving data across, and under Move flights from Drone Logbook choose all four files together.
  4. Check the preview. It shows, for each aircraft, how many flights are new, how many are already here (skipped), and any that need checking (no proper date or zero length, listed but not imported).
  5. If it looks right, click Import.

Your existing flights aren't changed, and running it again won't create duplicates. Drone Logbook's export doesn't include GPS tracks, so old flights come across without a map track.

Help us test a new aircraft: if you fly something marked "being tested", emailing Aspex two or three of its flight files, zipped, is the quickest way to get it fully supported.

Why battery details show for some drones and not others

Battery details come from the drone's own flight file. DJI drones record the battery's serial number, charge level, cell voltages, temperature and cycle count, so the logbook can show all of it. senseFly eBee files don't record the battery in a readable way, and Skydio Cloud only gives the battery's serial number. For those, you can add the battery to a flight yourself.

The Matrice 30, 300 and 350 fly on two batteries at once. For now, the logbook links each flight to one of the pair. Full two-battery support is coming.

The Logbook tab lists every flight, newest first, with summaries at the top.

Click a column heading to sort by it. Use the search box to find flights by date, flight number, battery or aircraft.

Opening a flight

Click a flight to open it. You'll see its track on the map, and you can download it for Google Earth.

Editing a flight's CASA details

In an open flight you can record:

  • Pilot in command: leave blank if it was you. Your name comes from the Me tab.
  • Other pilot / observer
  • Operation: for example VLOS or EVLOS. Your usual type is set on the Me tab.
  • Serviceable after flight, and any defect. Defects go into your technical log.
  • Notes
  • The job the flight was part of

Summaries

The panels at the top show your flights and hours for each aircraft and each battery, with battery health figures, plus your flight hours by month. Click an aircraft to show only its flights.

5. Jobs, Sites and Approvals

Sites

A site is a place you fly, such as a solar farm. Give it a name, and add its boundary by dropping in a Google Earth KML of the whole site. The logbook then knows its location and area in hectares, and flights that take off there are matched to it automatically.

Airspace check. Once a site has its boundary, click Check airspace. The logbook lists airspace over and near the site and aerodromes within 15 km, and gives a plain answer: no issues found, check before flying, or approval likely needed (for example inside a control zone or restricted area, or within 3 NM of an aerodrome). Jobs at that site show the same answer, with a button to mark the job as needing an approval. This is a preliminary check from community-maintained OpenAIP data, not official data. Always confirm it against official Airservices Australia data (for example in Airservices’ OpenSky app or another CASA-verified drone safety app) and check NOTAMs before flying.

Jobs

A job is a piece of work at a site, for a client: for example "Walla Walla thermography Nov 2026". Flights are linked to jobs, so you can see exactly what was flown for each one, and your CASA logs show what each flight was for.

Create jobs for past flights groups your existing flights by site and date. Tick the groups you want, click Create ticked jobs, and they become jobs in one go.

Aspex jobs

When you accept an Aspex job, it appears under Aspex jobs with what you need on site: the site contact (usually the O&M manager) and safety requirements such as SWMS, PPE, cones and a landing pad. Flights you fly on that job are linked to it, and those are the only flights Aspex can see.

Approvals

The Approvals tab keeps your approvals and permissions in one place: controlled airport authorisations, CASA approvals and instruments, restricted area permissions, BVLOS or EVLOS approvals, and landowner, council or national park permissions. For each one, record who issued it, the reference number, the dates it's valid, the area and maximum height, and its conditions. Attach the approval itself (a PDF, or a photo of it) so it's on hand on site.

The list shows which approvals are current, which haven't started yet and which have expired. Approvals expiring within 30 days are included in your Monday reminder email.

Linking an approval to a job. If a job needs an approval, open the job and tick This job needs an approval or permission. Then either click + New approval for this job, which fills in the job's dates and site for you, or choose one you already have from Link an existing approval. The job warns you if nothing linked covers its dates, and so does your reminder email.

Aspex jobs. Each pilot normally arranges their own approvals. If an Aspex job needs one, the job says so under Approvals. Click + Add my own approval for this job to record yours, and Aspex will see it and its document (not your private notes). If Aspex is arranging the approval for you, it appears on the job with its conditions and document once it's in.

6. Inventory

Drones, batteries and payloads

Everything you fly appears here automatically after its first imported flight. Give each item a short code you'll recognise, such as M3-1 for a battery, instead of a long serial number. Set its Source (genuine DJI or not) and its Status: in service or retired.

Identify a battery helps when you don't know which physical battery has which serial. Put a battery in the drone, switch on, and import. The battery in the most recent flight is shown, ready for you to give it a code.

Battery health

Each battery gets a colour, worked out from its flight records. The worst check decides the colour.

  • Good: capacity, cell balance, voltage and temperature all within normal limits.
  • Watch: losing capacity, cells drifting apart, running hot or flat, or not used for a long time. Still usable, but keep an eye on it. The Why column says what was found and what to do.
  • Replace / check: below 80% capacity, a badly imbalanced cell, or abused. Retire it or have it checked before relying on it.
  • Gauge not reporting: the battery reports exactly the same capacity every flight, so its real capacity can't be measured. That's typical of a non-standard battery. The other checks still apply.

Charge cycles and storage

The cycle count is read from the battery itself in each flight file. A cycle is roughly one full discharge, so two half flights make one cycle. Batteries are flagged at high cycle counts, and when they haven't been used for 3 months: give them a full charge, fly or discharge them, then return them to storage charge.

Maintenance

Each drone has a maintenance log. Set how often it should be inspected (every so many flight hours), and record each inspection item by item: propellers, arms, cameras, firmware checked or updated. Record parts replaced, too. Entries can be edited after saving, and a reminder is sent when an inspection is due.

7. CASA logs

CASA's Part 101 rules (MOS chapter 10) require records of your flying. The logbook builds them from your flights, so you don't have to keep them by hand.

LogWhat it records
Remote pilot logYour own flying: date, aircraft, flight time, day or night, operation type, pilot in command
RPAS operational logEach aircraft's flights: where, when, who flew it and for how long
RPAS technical logEach aircraft's serviceability, defects and maintenance

Producing a log

  1. Open the CASA logs tab.
  2. Pick the log and a date range.
  3. Click Print / save as PDF for a copy to keep or send, or Download CSV to open it in Excel.

Getting the details right

  • Day or night is worked out from the time and place of each flight.
  • Pilot in command is you, unless you've entered someone else on the flight.
  • Operation type uses your usual type from the Me tab, unless you've set it on the flight or its job.
  • Defects and "not serviceable" entries you record on flights appear in the technical log.

The logbook helps you keep your records, but keeping them correct and complete is still your responsibility as the pilot or operator.

8. Me (your details)

Fill this in first. It feeds your CASA logs and reminders.

  • Your full name and ARN
  • Your RePL number, class and expiry
  • Your insurance expiry
  • Other qualifications
  • Your usual operation type (for example VLOS)
  • Weekly reminder email: on or off

Reminders

With reminders on, you get an email on Mondays when something needs attention: your RePL, insurance, drone registration or an approval coming up for renewal, a job that needs an approval without one linked, a drone inspection due, or a battery wearing out or sitting unused too long. If nothing is due, no email is sent.

9. Troubleshooting

My controller shows no files on the PC

Switch the drone on as well as the controller and wait for them to link. Most DJI controllers only show the flight files while the drone is on. Also check you're using a data cable, not a charge-only cable, and choose File transfer if the controller asks.

The files I emailed arrived scrambled

DJI flight files end in .txt but aren't really text, and some email systems damage them. Zip them first: on Windows, right-click → Send to → Compressed (zipped) folder. On a Mac, right-click → Compress.

A battery shows no charge-cycle count

The count fills in from the battery's most recent flight. If a battery hasn't flown since you started using the logbook, it fills in next time it flies. You can also drop its latest flight file into Import flights again.

"Already in the logbook"

That flight is already logged, so it was skipped. Nothing is doubled up.

Windows warns me when I open Import Flights

That's normal the first time, because it's a downloaded program. In your browser, click Keep if asked. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway. It only asks once.

A flight shows no GPS track or battery details

The flight file may be encrypted and need the DJI developer key (see section 2). Flights brought across from Drone Logbook never have tracks. Drone Logbook's export doesn't include them.

My aircraft shows as "DJI aircraft (type …)"

It's newer than the logbook's flight-file reader. The flight is logged normally. Emailing Aspex a couple of its flight files, zipped, helps us give it its proper name.
